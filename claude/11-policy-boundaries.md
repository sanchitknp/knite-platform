# Policy boundaries

Things that look like shortcuts and are not. Recorded because each was considered and
rejected for a specific reason.

## Duplicate ASINs

Creating several listings for the same product to occupy more search slots is
**duplicate ASIN creation** — explicitly enforced against. Different titles, different
packaging or different barcodes do not make it a different product.

Consequences: listing suppression, ASIN removal, a violation recorded on **Account Health
for 180 days**, and at the extreme, account deactivation.

Account Health governs Featured Offer eligibility. A duplicate-listing flag risks the
Buy Box.

**What is legitimate:** genuinely different products — different membrane throughput,
different cartridge stack, different bundle contents, different tank size. The spec in
the listing must match something you can actually ship.

### Evidence from the category

RK Aqua Fresh India runs ~20 purifier listings. Their volume comes from **four old
listings** with 329–1,600 reviews. Everything launched recently sits at 1–4 reviews and
near-zero sales. Listing count is not what makes them work; an aged review base and an
83% discount off a ₹24,999 MRP is.

## Review manipulation

Unverified reviews from friends and family are detected by graph analysis — shared IPs,
devices, payment instruments, delivery addresses, account ages, and reviewers whose only
purchase from a new seller is your product.

Classified as review manipulation, which is an **account-level** violation, not a listing
one. Reviews get stripped (so the spend is wasted) and Account Health takes the hit.

**Legitimate routes:** Amazon Vine (up to 30 units/ASIN, costs inventory not cash),
"Request a Review" on every order, and a product insert that asks for a review without
offering anything in return.

## Unsubstantiated claims

Quantified performance claims need evidence you can produce. "Up to 45% water savings"
was declined because it was copied from a competitor listing and no measurement exists
for KNITE units. Same for "ISI Certified*" with an unexplained asterisk.

## Multiple warranty figures

At one point the warranty read 1 year on the website, 2 years on Amazon, and 4 years on
Drive artwork — for the same product. Warranty is a commitment, not copy. Keep one number
per model across every channel.
