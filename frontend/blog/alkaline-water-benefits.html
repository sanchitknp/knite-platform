Alkaline Water Explained: pH, Minerals & Daily Wellness

📅 5 min read· Updated June 2026

"Alkaline water" is one of the most marketed wellness trends of the last decade. Athletes drink it, celebrities post about it, and most premium RO purifiers in India now include an alkaline stage. But what is it actually, and is it worth the extra cost? This guide separates the hype from the science.

What Is Alkaline Water?

Water has a pH value on a scale from 0 (highly acidic) to 14 (highly alkaline), with 7 being neutral. Most regular drinking water sits around pH 6.5–7.5. Alkaline water has a pH of 8.0–9.5, achieved by adding mineral content that shifts the chemistry toward the basic end of the scale.

Alkaline RO purifiers achieve this through a mineraliser cartridge that adds trace amounts of calcium, magnesium, potassium, and bicarbonates back into the RO-purified water.

Two Types of Alkaline Water

1. Natural Mineral Alkaline (Recommended)

Water becomes alkaline naturally as it picks up dissolved minerals from rocks. Spring water from limestone aquifers, for instance, can be naturally pH 8.0+. Modern RO purifiers like the Knite Prime replicate this by passing purified water through a calcium-magnesium media bed.

2. Ionised / Electrolysed Alkaline

Some expensive devices use electrolysis to split water into acidic and alkaline streams. While effective, these are pricey, energy-intensive, and the long-term health benefits versus naturally mineralised water are not well established. Most experts recommend the mineral-based approach.

What Science Says About Alkaline Water

Research is mixed but trending positive. Studies in journals including the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition have found:

  • Better hydration during exercise — alkaline water may be absorbed slightly faster due to smaller cluster size
  • Reduced acid reflux symptoms — pH 8.8+ water deactivates pepsin, the enzyme that causes reflux damage
  • Improved blood viscosity — one study showed athletes had better hydration markers with alkaline water vs. regular water
  • Bone health support — alkaline water with calcium and magnesium contributes to daily mineral intake
  • Possible antioxidant effect — naturally alkaline water with trace minerals may have mild free-radical neutralising properties

What the science does not support:

  • "Curing" cancer or any disease (your body tightly regulates blood pH at 7.35–7.45 regardless of what you drink)
  • "Detoxifying" — your liver and kidneys already do this
  • Weight loss claims
The honest verdict: Alkaline mineralised water is a small but worthwhile improvement over plain RO output — primarily because it adds back beneficial minerals stripped by RO. It's not a miracle cure, but a quality-of-life upgrade.

Ideal pH Range for Drinking

The WHO recommends drinking water pH between 6.5 and 8.5. Alkaline RO output at pH 8.5–9.0 sits at the upper end of this range — beneficial without being extreme. Going significantly above 9.5 isn't advised, as the water can taste bitter and may interfere with stomach acid digestion.

How to Test Your Water's pH

  1. Buy a digital pH meter (₹400–800) or pH test strips (₹100 for 100 strips).
  2. Take a sample of your RO output water in a clean glass.
  3. Dip the electrode or strip into the water; wait 10 seconds.
  4. Reading between 8.0–9.0 → working correctly.
  5. Reading below 7.0 → alkaline cartridge needs replacement.
